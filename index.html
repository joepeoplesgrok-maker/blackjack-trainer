3. Why Card Counting Works (The Math)

Unlike roulette or slots, Blackjack has memory—cards already dealt are removed from the shoe until the next shuffle. Because rules treat players and dealers asymmetrically, a shoe rich in 10s and Aces shifts expected value in your favor:

  • 3:2 Blackjack Bonus: Both you and the dealer get more Blackjacks in high counts, but yours pays 1.5x while the dealer's only takes 1.0x. (Never play 6:5 tables—they add ~1.39% to house edge.)
  • Dealer Must Hit Stiffs (12–16): You can stand on 12–16; the dealer is forced to hit and busts far more often when the shoe is dense with 10s.
  • Doubling & Splitting Pay More: Doubling on 10 or 11 lands a 20 or 21 much more often in positive counts.
  • Deck Count & Penetration: Fewer decks reduce base house edge (2D S17/DAS ≈ -0.19% vs 6D ≈ -0.41% vs 8D ≈ -0.43%), while deeper cut-card penetration creates extreme True Counts much more often.
4. Running Count, Cancellation & True Count

Hi-Lo Tag Values

  • 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 → +1 (Low cards removed = stronger remaining shoe)
  • 7, 8, 9 → 0 (Neutral—ignore them completely)
  • 10, J, Q, K, A → -1 (High cards removed = weaker remaining shoe)

Pro Speed Technique: Pair Cancellation

Don't count cards one by one as they hit the felt. Wait until each player has two cards, then cancel pairs: a King (-1) and a 4 (+1) sum to 0—ignore both! Practice this in Speed Count Drill → 2 Cards (Pairs).

Converting to True Count

A Running Count of +6 with 6 decks left is diluted (+1.0 per deck). With 1.5 decks left in Double Deck or late in a 6D shoe, it's a monster (+4.0 per deck). Always divide by unseen remaining decks (including the dealer's hole card and burn card):

True Count (TC) = Running Count ÷ Decks Remaining

Every +1 TC shifts your advantage by roughly +0.50%.

5. Betting Strategies, Kelly Criterion & Wonging

Card counting generates ~80% of its profit from bet sizing and ~20% from playing deviations.

Bet Spreads by Game Type

  • Double Deck (2D): A 1–6 or 1–8 unit spread beats the ~0.19%–0.39% house edge cleanly without extreme casino heat.
  • 6-Deck & 8-Deck Shoes: Typically require a 1–12 or 1–16 unit spread to overcome the ~0.41%–0.65% base house edge.

The Kelly Criterion (Dynamic Bankroll Sizing)

Full Kelly Bet % = Edge ÷ Variance (σ² ≈ 1.33)
Half-Kelly Bet = Bankroll × 0.5 × (Edge ÷ 1.33)

Half-Kelly (selectable in ⚙ Rules → Betting Strategy) achieves 75% of the maximum theoretical growth rate while dramatically cutting your Risk of Ruin (RoR).

Wonging (Back-Counting)

Wonging In means sitting down only at TC ≥ +1/+2. Wonging Out means leaving whenever TC hits -2 or worse. Watch the HUD's WONG indicator in Table Play!

6. Advanced Concepts & Other Counting Systems

Spreading to Two Hands

At high True Counts (TC ≥ +2 or +3), playing two spots at ~75% of your single-hand Kelly bet each lowers covariance-adjusted variance while pushing more total EV onto the table.

Casino Heat & Cover Play

  • Avoid robotic 1-to-12 jumps right after a losing hand; ramp naturally after wins when possible.
  • Splitting 10s (I18 #4 & #5) is the fastest way to draw pit attention—many pros skip it for longevity.
  • Keep sessions under 45–60 minutes per shift.

Other Counting Systems

  • KO (Knock-Out): Unbalanced count (7s are +1) that eliminates True Count division entirely.
  • Zen Count & Omega II: Level-2 balanced systems (+2/+1/0/-1/-2) gaining ~5–10% higher playing efficiency than Hi-Lo.
  • Hi-Opt II + Ace Side Count: Elite accuracy for single/double-deck pitch games.

    How It Works

    A plain-language guide to every strategy and feature in this trainer. Indices and formulas match the code. Open Settings (Rules) to turn any advanced feature on or off.

    The modes

    Table Play is a full blackjack table: you bet, play hands, and can use the count HUD, coach, and quizzes.

    Speed Count flashes cards from a 52-card deck so you can practice the running count against a timer.

    Strategy Drill quizzes you on the next play (hit, stand, double, split, surrender) for a random hand.

    Charts shows basic strategy and the deviation tables for your current rules.

    Stats tracks accuracy and bankroll and lets you review missed hands.

    Risk Tools estimates win rate, risk of ruin, and Kelly bet size for your spread.

    This How It Works page is always available.

    Basic strategy

    Basic strategy is the play that loses the least (or wins the most) in the long run for your rules, without using the count. This app follows Wizard of Odds total-dependent charts for 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8 decks, including S17 vs H17, DAS, and late surrender.

    Natural blackjack pays 3:2. The dealer peeks for blackjack (American style). Split aces get one card each and cannot be resplit. A two-card 21 after a split is not paid as blackjack.

    Hi-Lo running count, true count, and rounding

    Low cards 2–6 are +1, 7–9 are 0, and 10s and aces are −1. The running count (RC) is the sum of every seen card, including other seats when that feature is on. A burned card after the shuffle is not counted.

    The true count (TC) is RC divided by remaining decks (unseen cards ÷ 52), including the hidden hole card until it is revealed.

    In Settings you can apply a rounding convention everywhere (HUD, deviations, bets, quizzes):

    • Exact (default) — the raw fraction, same as v6. Displayed to one decimal.
    • Floor — always rounded down (3.9 → 3, −1.1 → −2).
    • Truncate — toward zero (3.9 → 3, −1.1 → −1).
    • Round — nearest integer (3.5 → 4, −1.5 → −1 in JavaScript Math.round).
    Illustrious 18 (6-deck style set)

    When the I18 toggle is on, the coach and drills use these count-based changes. Multi-deck insurance is typically TC ≥ +3 (1-deck +1.5, 2-deck +2.5). The 2-deck “9 vs 2” deviation is now a true-count play: hit when TC < 0 (it used to use the running count). H17 and double-deck use the usual index shifts already in the charts (11 vs A, 10 vs A).

    The 18 playing deviations (besides insurance, listed as #1 on the Charts tab): 16 vs 10 stand TC ≥ 0; 15 vs 10 stand TC ≥ +4; split 10s vs 5 at +5 and vs 6 at +4; double 10 vs 10 at +4; 12 vs 3 stand at +2; 12 vs 2 stand at +3; 11 vs A double at +1 (6D S17) or hit at ≤ −1 when basic is double; 9 vs 2 double at +1 (6D) or hit at TC < 0 (2D); 10 vs A double at +4 (6D S17) or +3 (2D/H17); 9 vs 7 double at +3; 16 vs 9 stand at +5; 13 vs 2 hit at ≤ −1; 12 vs 4 hit at ≤ 0; 12 vs 5 hit at ≤ −2; 12 vs 6 hit at ≤ −1; 13 vs 3 hit at ≤ −2.

    Fab 4 late surrender

    When the Fab 4 toggle is on and late surrender is allowed: 14 vs 10 (including 7,7) surrender at TC ≥ +3; 15 vs 10 at TC ≥ 0; 15 vs 9 at TC ≥ +2; 15 vs A at TC ≥ +1 for S17, or TC ≥ −1 for H17.

    Insurance and even money

    Insurance (and even money, which is the same decision when you have blackjack vs an ace) is +EV at the insurance index: TC ≥ +3 in 4–8 deck shoes, +2.5 in double deck, +1.5 in single deck. When the insurance-index toggle is off, the coach does not grade this decision. A lost or won insurance bet is included in the round’s net result.

    Bet spreads and the ramp

    You set a unit size and a min–max spread (for example 1–8 or 1–12). The default ramp bets the minimum at TC ≤ 1, then adds one unit per true count, capped at the maximum. The HUD can show the recommended bet. Optional bet grading scores each deal as correct, too high, or too low against that ramp. You can still pick Half-Kelly in Betting Strategy if you prefer a bankroll-fraction bet.

    Quizzes and drills

    Count-check quizzes pause Table Play after a set number of hands, or at a random gap between 5 and 15 hands. Enter the running count and true count. The true-count answer uses your rounding setting (in Exact mode, either truncate or round is accepted, as before).

    Live shoe and discard bars sit beside the table during Table Play (on by default). The Shoe bar empties and the Discard bar fills as cards come out, including other seats, and they reset on a shuffle. A red mark on the Shoe bar is the cut card. Exact numbers stay hidden unless you turn on “Show exact decks remaining.” Tap the bars at any time and estimate decks remaining; a guess within 0.5 decks is correct. Feedback shows the actual decks left and how your guess would have changed the true count. An optional prompt can ask every 5, 10, or 15 hands (off by default).

    Deck-estimation drill (a separate toggle) is a standalone quiz: a practice tray is shown at a random depth and you guess decks remaining or already played, also within 0.5 decks.

    Speed Count times a full 52-card run. You enter the final RC (always 0 for a full deck). Time and accuracy are scored, and your best correct time is saved.

    Timed decisions in Strategy Drill give you a set number of seconds. If the timer runs out, the hand is scored as a miss.

    Stats and mistake review

    When stats tracking is on, the app records session and lifetime accuracy for hard totals, soft totals, pairs, deviations, bets, and insurance, plus a bankroll history chart and your most-missed matchups. Review mistakes replays logged errors as strategy quizzes. Data stays in this browser (versioned localStorage) and the mistake log is capped. Use Reset stats & logs to clear it.

    Win rate, risk of ruin, and Kelly

    Risk Tools estimates expected win per hour, standard deviation per hour, N0 (hands for a 1-standard-deviation edge), and risk of ruin for your bankroll, using your spread, rules, and penetration. True-count frequencies are simulated with this app’s own shuffle and cut card and then cached. Kelly and half-Kelly bet sizes are shown as a fraction of bankroll. Every result is labeled as an estimate.

    Other seats and heat hints

    You can add 1–6 other seats that play basic strategy from the same shoe. Their cards are dealt and counted in the running count, which changes true counts and penetration the way a full table does. They do not use deviations and do not affect your bankroll.

    Heat hints are educational only. If a bet jumps more than 2×, or you leap from a small bet to the top of the spread, you will see a reminder that ramping with the count is quieter than jumping after a win or loss. Nothing is blocked.

    What each toggle does

    Open Rules → Features. Turning a toggle off hides that tool’s screens and stops its logic. The core table, basic strategy, and charts remain. How It Works and Table Play always stay in the mode list. Each Settings → Features control:

    • Illustrious 18 — coach and drills use the I18 indexes. Off: those plays follow basic strategy.
    • Fab 4 — late-surrender deviations (14/15 vs 9, 10, Ace). Off: those plays follow basic strategy.
    • Insurance index — grades Insurance / Even Money at the deck-aware count. Off: no insurance grade.
    • TC rounding — exact, floor, truncate, or round; used by the HUD, deviations, quizzes, and the bet ramp.
    • Bet ramp — HUD recommended bet follows the TC → units ramp.
    • Grade bets — after you Deal, the bet is marked correct, too high, or too low against the ramp, shown next to the recommended bet until you change the wager or deal again.
    • Count-check quizzes — optional RC/TC prompts during Table Play. Quiz frequency sets every N hands or a random 5–15 gap.
    • Live shoe & discard bars (on by default) — the two bars on the table; turn it off to hide them and disable tap-to-estimate and auto-prompts.
    • Show exact decks remaining (off by default) — labels the bars with the true leftover decks.
    • Deck-estimate prompt — asks you to guess leftover decks every N hands (off by default).
    • Deck-estimation drill — standalone practice quiz (does not control the live bars).
    • Speed Count mode and Strategy Drill mode — show or hide those modes in the header menu.
    • Timed decisions — countdown in Strategy Drill; a timeout is scored as a miss. Seconds per decision sets the limit.
    • Stats and Risk Tools — show or hide those modes and (for Stats) stop logging.
    • Other seats — 1–6 basic-strategy seats that share the shoe and the count.
    • Heat hints — an educational warning when a bet jumps more than 2× or leaps from a small wager to the top of the spread.
